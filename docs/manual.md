# Reselling Basics

**A beginner manual for finding items to flip, writing listings, pricing, shipping, fees, and record keeping on eBay and Amazon.**

Published by Asdale Dynamics, LLC (Indiana, formed October 2026).
Plain English. No experience needed.

---

## Introduction

Reselling (also called flipping) means buying something at one price and selling it for more. You don't need a warehouse, a business degree, or special software. You need a few hours a week, a place to store items, and a way to ship them.

This manual walks you through the whole process, step by step:

1. Finding items to flip
2. Writing listings that sell
3. Pricing for profit
4. Shipping supplies and packing
5. eBay and Amazon fees
6. Record keeping

Print the checklist and worksheet at the end and keep them by your workspace.

---

## 0. Before you list: set up your seller accounts

You need a place to sell. Pick one platform to start — eBay or Amazon — and add the other later.

### eBay

1. Create a free eBay account at ebay.com.
2. Add a payment method (PayPal or a bank account) so eBay can pay you.
3. Review eBay's seller fees and prohibited items list before you list anything.
4. Start as an individual seller. You can open an eBay Store later if your sales justify it.

### Amazon

1. Create a seller account at sellercentral.amazon.com.
2. Choose the **Individual plan** ($0.99 per item sold) if you expect fewer than about 40 sales a month, or the **Professional plan** ($39.99/month) if you expect more.
3. Provide your bank account and tax information so Amazon can pay you.
4. Review Amazon's referral fees and category restrictions before you list.

### Either way

- Use an email address you check regularly.
- Have a bank account ready for payouts.
- Do not list anything until you have read that platform's current fee and policy pages — they change.

## 2. Finding items to flip

Start with what you already know. If you know clothes, sell clothes. If you know books, sell books. Familiar categories help you spot a good deal quickly.

### Where to look

- **Your own home.** Closets, garages, and drawers are free inventory. Sell what you no longer use first.
- **Thrift stores and charity shops.** Clothes, books, kitchenware, toys, and small electronics.
- **Garage and estate sales.** Often priced to move, especially on the last day.
- **Clearance racks.** Retail stores mark down seasonal or discontinued items.
- **Online marketplaces.** Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, and OfferUp often have local pickup deals.

### What sells well

Look for items that are:

- Small and light (cheap to ship)
- Easy to test (turns on, no missing parts)
- In recognizable brands
- Not fragile or hazardous
- Legal to resell (no counterfeit goods, no recalled items, no restricted categories without approval)

### Avoid

- Items you cannot test
- Anything broken unless you know how to fix it and the math still works
- Counterfeit or brand-knockoff items
- Hazardous materials (batteries that leak, chemicals, aerosols)
- Anything you are not allowed to sell on eBay or Amazon

### Research before you buy

Before you buy, search completed sales on eBay (filter: Sold listings) and current listings on Amazon to see what similar items actually sell for. If you cannot find recent sales, do not buy the item.

## 3. Writing listings that sell

A good listing answers every question a buyer might have before they ask.

### Title

Keep it clear and specific. Include:

- Brand
- Model or item name
- Size, color, or condition (when it matters)
- Key details (e.g., "new with tags," "works," "lot of 5")

Bad: "Nice shoes"
Good: "Nike Air Max running shoes, men's size 10, black, lightly worn"

### Photos

Take photos in natural light, against a plain background. Show:

- The front, back, and sides
- Any flaws (scratches, stains, wear)
- Labels, tags, and serial numbers
- The item working, if it powers on

More photos build trust. Buyers buy what they can see.

### Description

Write in short sentences. Include:

- What the item is
- Condition (new, like new, good, acceptable)
- What works and what does not
- What comes in the box
- Measurements, if clothing or furniture
- Shipping speed and cost
- Returns policy (if you accept returns)

Be honest. Honest listings get fewer returns and better feedback.

### Keywords

Use words a buyer would search for. If you are selling a coffee maker, include "coffee maker," "coffee machine," "12 cup," and the brand name in the title or description.

## 4. Pricing for profit

Price = what you sell for minus what you spend. Profit is what is left.

### The profit formula

```
Sale price
- Item cost
- Shipping supplies
- Postage
- eBay/Amazon fees
- Taxes set aside
= Your profit
```

If the number is zero or negative, do not buy or list the item.

### Research first

Check sold listings on eBay and current prices on Amazon. Price near recent sales, not near dream listings that have been sitting for months.

### Start lower, not higher

As a beginner, price slightly below similar items to sell quickly and build feedback. You can raise prices later once you have reviews.

### Factor in everything

A $10 item that costs $3 to ship and $1 in fees leaves $6 profit — before taxes. Be realistic about every cost.

## 5. Shipping supplies and packing

Good packing protects your item and your feedback score.

### Basic supplies

- Boxes and padded envelopes
- Bubble wrap or packing paper
- Packing tape
- Shipping labels (print at home or at the carrier)
- Scale (a kitchen scale works for small items)
- Printer (optional; carriers can print labels)

### Where to get supplies

- Free boxes from USPS, UPS, or FedEx (for their services)
- Reuse clean boxes from home or stores
- Buy bulk bubble wrap and tape online or at office stores

### Packing tips

- Wrap fragile items in bubble wrap
- Fill empty space so the item cannot shift
- Tape boxes shut on all seams
- Tape the label securely, or put it in a clear sleeve
- Take a photo of the packed item before sealing, in case of a dispute

### Shipping carriers

- **USPS:** Good for small, light items. First Class Package and Priority Mail.
- **UPS / FedEx:** Often better for heavier packages.
- **Pirate Ship / ShipStation:** Free tools to print discounted labels at home.

Always ship with tracking. Never ship without it.

## 6. eBay and Amazon fees

Both platforms take a cut of every sale. Know the numbers before you price.

### eBay

Common fees include:

- **Final value fee:** A percentage of the total sale (item price + shipping), usually around 10–15% depending on category and your store level.
- **Payment processing fee:** Around 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.
- **Listing fees:** eBay gives a certain number of free listings per month; beyond that, a small fee per listing.

Check eBay's current fee page. Fees change.

### Amazon

- **Referral fee:** A percentage of the sale price, varies by category (often 8–15%).
- **Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) fees:** If you send inventory to Amazon warehouses, they charge storage and fulfillment fees.
- **Individual plan:** $0.99 per item sold.
- **Professional plan:** $39.99 per month, no per-item fee. Worth it if you sell more than about 40 items a month.

### Example

You sell a $30 item with $6 shipping on eBay:

- Item + shipping: $36
- eBay fees (~13%): about $4.70
- Payment processing (~2.9% + $0.30): about $1.35
- Your cost for the item: $8
- Shipping supplies: $1
- Postage: $6
- Left over: about $15 — before taxes

## 7. Record keeping

Track every purchase and every sale. You need this for taxes and to know if you are actually making money.

### What to record

For each item:

- Date bought
- Where bought
- Cost
- Date listed
- Platform (eBay or Amazon)
- Sale price
- Shipping cost
- Fees paid
- Profit
- Buyer and item details (in case of returns)

### Tools

- A notebook or printed worksheet
- A spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel, or LibreOffice)
- Free accounting software (Wave, etc.)

### Taxes

Money you make from reselling may be taxable income. Keep receipts and records, and talk to a tax professional about what you owe. This guide is education only, not tax advice.

## 8. Your first 7 days

A simple week-by-week plan so you do not get overwhelmed.

**Day 1:** Set up your seller account (eBay or Amazon). Read that platform's fee and policy pages.
**Day 2:** Look through your own home. Pick 3–5 items you no longer use and photograph them.
**Day 3:** Research those items on sold listings. Decide sale prices using the profit formula.
**Day 4:** List your first 3 items. Use the listing checklist for each one.
**Day 5:** Source 5–10 more items from a thrift store or garage sale. Research before you buy.
**Day 6:** Pack and ship anything that sold. Update your record-keeping sheet.
**Day 7:** Review your profit worksheet. Decide what to keep doing and what to change.

If you sell nothing in week one, that is normal. Keep listing and researching — feedback and sales build with time.
