Reselling Basics

A beginner manual for finding items to flip, writing listings, pricing, shipping supplies, fees, and record keeping on eBay and Amazon.

Published by Asdale Dynamics, LLC (Indiana, formed October 2026). Plain English. No experience needed.

Start with what you know. If you know clothes, sell clothes. If you know books, sell books. Familiar categories help you spot a good deal quickly.

1. Finding items to flip

Reselling (also called flipping) means buying something at one price and selling it for more. You do not need a warehouse, a business degree, or special software. You need a few hours a week, a place to store items, and a way to ship them.

Where to look

  • Your own home. Closets, garages, and drawers are free inventory. Sell what you no longer use first.
  • Thrift stores and charity shops. Clothes, books, kitchenware, toys, and small electronics.
  • Garage and estate sales. Often priced to move, especially on the last day.
  • Clearance racks. Retail stores mark down seasonal or discontinued items.
  • Online marketplaces. Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, and OfferUp often have local pickup deals.

What sells well

  • Small and light (cheap to ship)
  • Easy to test (turns on, no missing parts)
  • Recognizable brands
  • Not fragile or hazardous
  • Legal to resell (no counterfeit goods, no recalled items, no restricted categories without approval)

Avoid

  • Items you cannot test
  • Anything broken unless you know how to fix it and the math still works
  • Counterfeit or brand-knockoff items
  • Hazardous materials (batteries that leak, chemicals, aerosols)
  • Anything you are not allowed to sell on eBay or Amazon

Research before you buy. Before you buy, search completed sales on eBay (filter: Sold listings) and current listings on Amazon to see what similar items actually sell for. If you cannot find recent sales, do not buy the item.

2. Writing listings

A good listing answers every question a buyer might have before they ask.

Title

Keep it clear and specific. Include brand, model or item name, size/color/condition (when it matters), and key details (e.g., "new with tags," "works," "lot of 5").

Bad: "Nice shoes"
Good: "Nike Air Max running shoes, men's size 10, black, lightly worn"

Photos

Take photos in natural light, against a plain background. Show the front, back, and sides; any flaws (scratches, stains, wear); labels, tags, and serial numbers; and the item working, if it powers on. More photos build trust.

Description

Write in short sentences. Include what the item is, condition (new, like new, good, acceptable), what works and what does not, what comes in the box, measurements (if clothing or furniture), shipping speed and cost, and your returns policy. Be honest — honest listings get fewer returns and better feedback.

Keywords

Use words a buyer would search for. If you are selling a coffee maker, include "coffee maker," "coffee machine," "12 cup," and the brand name in the title or description.

3. Pricing for profit

Price = what you sell for minus what you spend. Profit is what is left.

The profit formula

StepWhat to subtract
Sale priceStart here
− Item costWhat you paid for the item
− Shipping suppliesBox, bubble wrap, tape
− PostageCost to ship
− eBay/Amazon feesPlatform and payment fees
− Taxes set asideEstimated tax on profit
= Your profitWhat you actually keep

If the number is zero or negative, do not buy or list the item.

Research first

Check sold listings on eBay and current prices on Amazon. Price near recent sales, not near dream listings that have been sitting for months.

Start lower, not higher

As a beginner, price slightly below similar items to sell quickly and build feedback. You can raise prices later once you have reviews.

4. Shipping supplies and packing

Good packing protects your item and your feedback score.

Basic supplies

  • Boxes and padded envelopes
  • Bubble wrap or packing paper
  • Packing tape
  • Shipping labels (print at home or at the carrier)
  • Scale (a kitchen scale works for small items)
  • Printer (optional; carriers can print labels)

Where to get supplies

  • Free boxes from USPS, UPS, or FedEx (for their services)
  • Reuse clean boxes from home or stores
  • Buy bulk bubble wrap and tape online or at office stores

Packing tips

  • Wrap fragile items in bubble wrap
  • Fill empty space so the item cannot shift
  • Tape boxes shut on all seams
  • Tape the label securely, or put it in a clear sleeve
  • Take a photo of the packed item before sealing, in case of a dispute

Shipping carriers

  • USPS: Good for small, light items. First Class Package and Priority Mail.
  • UPS / FedEx: Often better for heavier packages.
  • Pirate Ship / ShipStation: Free tools to print discounted labels at home.

Always ship with tracking. Never ship without it.

5. eBay and Amazon fees

Both platforms take a cut of every sale. Know the numbers before you price.

eBay

  • Final value fee: A percentage of the total sale (item price + shipping), usually around 10–15% depending on category and your store level.
  • Payment processing fee: Around 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.
  • Listing fees: eBay gives a certain number of free listings per month; beyond that, a small fee per listing.

Amazon

  • Referral fee: A percentage of the sale price, varies by category (often 8–15%).
  • Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) fees: If you send inventory to Amazon warehouses, they charge storage and fulfillment fees.
  • Individual plan: $0.99 per item sold.
  • Professional plan: $39.99 per month, no per-item fee. Worth it if you sell more than about 40 items a month.

Check eBay's and Amazon's current fee pages. Fees change.

Example

You sell a $30 item with $6 shipping on eBay:

  • Item + shipping: $36
  • eBay fees (~13%): about $4.70
  • Payment processing (~2.9% + $0.30): about $1.35
  • Your cost for the item: $8
  • Shipping supplies: $1
  • Postage: $6
  • Left over: about $15 — before taxes

6. Record keeping

Track every purchase and every sale. You need this for taxes and to know if you are actually making money.

What to record

  • Date bought
  • Where bought
  • Cost
  • Date listed
  • Platform (eBay or Amazon)
  • Sale price
  • Shipping cost
  • Fees paid
  • Profit
  • Buyer and item details (in case of returns)

Tools

  • A notebook or printed worksheet
  • A spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel, or LibreOffice)
  • Free accounting software (Wave, etc.)

Taxes: Money you make from reselling may be taxable income. Keep receipts and records, and talk to a tax professional about what you owe. This guide is education only, not tax advice.
